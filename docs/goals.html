About

Each gate is a finish time on a flat course at sea level. Gates are judged conservatively: a race passes when its clock time beats the gate time at its altitude, converted by whichever published method gives the least adjustment (Daniels' 4 or 5 s/mi per 1,000 ft above 3,000 ft, or the NCAA 2009 tables), and hills earn nothing. So a gate passed at altitude or on hills is one you'd certainly have passed on a flat course at sea level. Predictions and VDOTs on this page are the Fitness tab's (clock time, the methods averaged). The bars fill as that VDOT approaches the one the gate time works out to, from 10 points below.

The mileage goal is tracked against an even pace through the year (goal × days so far ÷ days in the year). "Needed" is the weekly average the rest of the year takes; the projection adds the planned miles in plan.csv, then the last 28 days' average, to what's done. Mileage counts runs only (hand-made FIT files too), never hikes or skis.
